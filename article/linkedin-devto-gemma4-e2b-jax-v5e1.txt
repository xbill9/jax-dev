Gemma 4 E2B in JAX on One TPU v5e: bf16 or 4-Bit, Python or Rust, and How It Stands Against vLLM

Gemma 4 E2B served three ways on one Cloud TPU v5e chip: a pure-JAX engine in Python, the same graph written in Rust and compiled by the libtpu that JAX loads, and vLLM, all timed by one client. bf16 beats 4-bit in the JAX engine, the Rust engine matches the JAX engine's tokens, and over HTTP it decodes at vLLM's rate with the fastest first token.

What is in it:

- 🟢 The Rust engine, the same graph built from Rust and compiled by JAX's own libtpu, decodes at vLLM's rate (0.998x) with the fastest first token, 8.9 ms
- 🟢 It matches the JAX engine: same argmax and top 10, KL 1.31e-6, 7 of 8 completions token for token
- 🟢 bf16 E2B fits a v5e chip (HBM 9.260 / 16.909 GB in use) and decodes 1.29x faster than 4-bit in the JAX engine
- ⚠️ The Python JAX engine decodes at 0.852x vLLM over HTTP; where its time goes was not measured
